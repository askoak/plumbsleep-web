How Plumb works

The sleeve is a viscose-made-from-bamboo knit, about five inches tall, worn on the upper thigh. Silicone dot grippers on the interior top rim keep it from sliding down the leg. The sleeve holds nothing inside it — it's a structural anchor, not a container.

The pillow is a contoured hourglass foam piece, about twelve inches long, seven inches wide, and five inches thick. It sits between the thighs the way a familiar knee pillow does. Open-cell polyurethane, CertiPUR-US certified.

The cover encases the pillow. Same viscose-made-from-bamboo knit as the sleeve. Removable for washing. The covered pillow attaches to the sleeve at the inner-thigh contact zone; how it attaches — magnetic, snap, or sleeve-mounted pouch — is what the current wear test is selecting.

One sleeve, one covered pillow, one leg. Designed to feel like premium sleepwear, not a medical brace.

One shape, two thicknesses

The pillow is a familiar hourglass silhouette — the same general shape side sleepers have used for decades — sized at twelve inches long and seven inches wide. Two thickness SKUs at launch: five inches for most adults, six inches for larger frames or firmer mattresses.

For larger frames: If you're over six feet or over 220 pounds — or if a Contour Legacy or similar hourglass pillow has felt too thin in the gap between your thighs — the six-inch pillow paired with the XL sleeve is designed for you. The thicker profile spans a wider inter-thigh gap and resists compression on firm mattresses.

For petite frames: If you've tried strap-on padded knee sleeves and found they gape on slim thighs, the Regular sleeve is cut for a smaller upper-thigh circumference. The pillow stays in place because the sleeve does — not because anything has to be tightened against your skin.

What we expect it to feel like

We won't know for certain until the wear test finishes, but here's the design intent: the sleeve should disappear from awareness the way a favorite pair of socks does. Soft knit, wide enough to spread pressure across the upper thigh rather than concentrating it like a strap, with enough stretch that you can pull it on without thinking and forget it's there. The covered pillow adds a small amount of bulk between the thighs — about what a folded knee pillow already does — held in place by the sleeve rather than left loose on the mattress. That's the bet. The wear test is how we find out if we got it right.

Built to fit a range of bodies

Plumb is sized along two axes. The sleeve comes in Regular or XL, based on upper-thigh circumference. The pillow comes in five-inch or six-inch thickness, based on body size and mattress firmness. We pair them at order time so the product fits more side sleepers across more body sizes and life stages — including bodies that are changing.

How Plumb differs from what's already out there

Knee pillows have been on the market for decades. Contour Legacy, Cushy Form, ComfiLife and others sell loose hourglass pillows for $25–$40. Companies like paDjamas, AliMed, and PalliPartners sell padded knee sleeves with cushions sewn inside for $35. We've used and studied all of them.

Loose pillows are soft and comfortable but fall out. Padded sleeves stay in place but tend to feel medical and can't be properly washed. Plumb separates the jobs across three pieces. The sleeve does the staying-in-place job. The pillow is freed to be optimized for comfort without compromising for a strap. The cover decouples wash durability from foam selection — you launder the cover, the foam airs out, and any one piece can be replaced as its own SKU when it eventually wears. That's an honest design choice, not a marketing line: foam compression and fabric wear are real, and treating each component as replaceable means you're not re-buying the whole product when one piece reaches the end of its useful life.

We think that's a better trade. We'll find out when testers wear it.

What we're testing

Plumb is pre-prototype. Before we tool, source, or ship anything, we're answering five questions through a 30-night wear test:

  • Does the sleeve stay positioned on the upper thigh? A sleeve can rotate around the thigh during sleep. Silicone grippers stop vertical slide, but rotation is the open question. Time-lapse video answers it.
  • Which attachment between the sleeve and the covered pillow works? Three concepts — magnetic, snap, or sleeve-mounted pouch — advance to this wear test. The one that holds through the night, releases one-handed at 3 a.m., and survives wash cycles wins. The other two don't ship.
  • Is it tolerable to wear? Sleep products fail at 2 a.m., not in a store. Tightness, heat, edge pressure, bunching — any one of them kills repeat use.
  • Do three pieces wash on three protocols without breaking the product? Sleeve machine-washes. Cover machine-washes with the foam removed. The foam pillow airs out and is never washed. We're confirming each piece survives thirty cycles and the attachment hardware survives with them.
  • Would you reach for it on night 14? Novelty is easy. Habit is the test.

We'll publish what we learn. If the sleeve rotates or no attachment mechanism clears the bar, we redesign or kill the idea. If the test passes, the next gate is 30 strangers, not friends.

Honest answers

How is this different from a $35 knee pillow sleeve?

Padded sleeves on the market put the cushion inside the sleeve, against the inner thigh of the worn leg — and can't be properly washed because the foam is sewn in. Plumb is three separate pieces: the sleeve, a removable washable cover, and a contoured foam pillow. The covered pillow attaches to the sleeve, so it stays in the inter-thigh gap rather than being squeezed against your own skin — and every piece can be washed or replaced independently. That's the bet. Whether it actually works better is what the wear test will tell us.

Will it cut off circulation?

The sleeve is soft knit and stretches significantly — closer to the feel of a wide compression sock than a strap. The grip comes from silicone dots, not from tightness. If a tester reports any numbness, tingling, or skin marks, we redesign. That's a hard kill criterion.

What happens when I roll over?

Honest answer: we don't fully know yet, and we're testing it. Here's the design response: the sleeve is a structural anchor on the upper thigh, about five inches tall, with multi-row silicone dot grippers on the interior top rim — the grippers engage skin or sleepwear to resist both vertical slide and rotation. The covered pillow attaches to the sleeve at the inner-thigh contact zone; the attachment mechanism (magnetic, snap, or sleeve-mounted pouch) is what the wear test is selecting. None of this is proven yet — the wear test answers it with time-lapse video and nightly logs. If the sleeve rotates or no attachment mechanism clears the bar, the plan is to redesign, not to ship something that doesn't work.

Which leg do I wear it on?

The dominant leg — the one that ends up on top more often when you're on your preferred side. Wear it on the leg that most often rests on top.

How tight is it?

Snug enough to stay in place; loose enough that you forget you're wearing it. The sleeve is wide enough on the upper thigh to distribute pressure across a wide area rather than concentrating it in a strap. The covered pillow doesn't tighten anything against your skin — it attaches to the sleeve, not to you.

Is it washable?

Most knee pillows are one foam block you can never wash. Plumb is built so the parts that touch you wash, and the part that doesn't, doesn't. Three pieces, three protocols. The sleeve machine-washes cold and air-dries flat, like any knit garment. The cover comes off the pillow and machine-washes the same way. The foam pillow itself is never washed — it airs out between uses and is designed to be replaced as a separate part when it eventually wears, rather than forcing you to re-buy the whole product. We chose three pieces specifically so wash durability doesn't fight foam selection.

Does it sleep hot?

The sleeve and cover are a viscose-made-from-bamboo knit, targeted at the breathability profile typical of viscose-blend sleepwear. Per FTC textile labeling guidance, the fiber is described as "viscose made from bamboo," not "bamboo" alone. Third-party thermal testing is planned before launch, so we're not going to make a quantitative heat claim yet. What we can say: anything worn on the leg adds some thermal mass, and the wear test will tell us how much.

How do I pick a size?

Two sleeve sizes — Regular (up to 28 in. upper thigh circumference) and XL (28–32 in.). Measure around the fullest part of your upper thigh, about three inches below the crease. If you're between sizes, size down — the sleeve needs to be snug enough for the silicone dot grippers to engage. If you're below the Regular range or above the XL cap (32 in.), email us before ordering and we'll talk through fit. Pillow thickness is a separate choice based on body size and mattress firmness — five inches for most adults, six inches for larger frames or firmer mattresses.

Plumb is a sleep-positioning product designed for comfort. It is not a medical device. It is not intended to diagnose, treat, mitigate, or cure any condition. Many side sleepers find a knee pillow more useful during pregnancy, and Plumb is designed to stay in place better than a loose pillow. We don't make any pregnancy-specific health claims; talk to your provider about what's appropriate for you.
