How Plumb works

The sleeve is a viscose-made-from-bamboo knit, at least 3 inches wide, with silicone dot grippers on the inside to keep it from sliding down the leg. A contoured hourglass cushion is held in an integrated pouch on the outside of the sleeve. Its concave base wraps the sleeve; its convex face contacts the opposite thigh. When you roll to your side, the cushion seats naturally in the gap between the thighs, with both legs gripping it laterally.

One sleeve, one cushion, one leg. Designed to feel like premium sleepwear, not a medical brace.

One shape, three thicknesses

The cushion is a familiar hourglass silhouette — the same general shape side sleepers have used for decades — refined to seat in the inter-thigh gap and pair with the sleeve.

For larger frames: If you're over 6 feet or over 220 pounds — or if a Contour Legacy or similar hourglass pillow has felt too small in the gap between your thighs — the 6-inch cushion paired with the XL sleeve is designed for you. The larger profile is meant to span a wider inter-thigh gap and resist compression on firm mattresses.

For petite frames: If you've tried strap-on padded knee sleeves and found they gape on slim thighs, the Regular sleeve is cut for a smaller upper-thigh circumference. The cushion is held in an integrated pouch on the outside of the sleeve, so the product doesn't depend on a tight, medical-feeling fit to work.

What we expect it to feel like

We won't know for certain until the wear test finishes, but here's the design intent: the sleeve should disappear from awareness the way a favorite pair of socks does. Soft knit, wide enough to spread pressure across the upper thigh rather than concentrating it like a strap, with enough stretch that you can pull it on without thinking and forget it's there. The cushion adds a small amount of bulk to the outside of one leg — about the weight of a folded washcloth — that you should notice for the first night or two and then stop noticing. That's the bet. The wear test is how we find out if we got it right.

Built to fit a range of bodies

Plumb pairs two components: a soft knit thigh sleeve (viscose made from bamboo) in Regular, Large, or XL, and a cushion in one of three thicknesses. The sleeve fits the upper thigh; the cushion seats in the inter-thigh gap. We pair them at order time rather than locking a fixed combination into every box, so the product fits more side sleepers across more body sizes and life stages — including bodies that are changing.

How Plumb differs from what's already out there

Knee pillows have been on the market for decades. Contour Legacy, Cushy Form, ComfiLife and others sell loose hourglass cushions for $25–$40. Companies like paDjamas, AliMed, and PalliPartners sell padded knee sleeves with cushions sewn inside for $35. We've used and studied all of them.

Plumb sits in between those two approaches. Loose pillows are soft and comfortable but fall out. Padded sleeves stay in place but tend to feel medical. Plumb holds the cushion in an integrated pouch on the exterior of a soft knit sleeve, so the cushion stays in position but isn't pressed against the worn leg's skin. We think that's a better trade. We'll find out when testers wear it.

What we're testing

Plumb is pre-prototype. Before we tool, source, or ship anything, we're answering four questions through a 30-night wear test:

  • Does the cushion stay in the inter-thigh gap? A sleeve can rotate around the thigh during sleep. Silicone grippers stop vertical slide, but rotation is the open question. Time-lapse video answers it.
  • Is it tolerable to wear? Sleep products fail at 2 a.m., not in a store. Tightness, heat, edge pressure, bunching — any one of them kills repeat use.
  • Does it work on both sides? Side sleepers roll. The single-sleeve design has to work when you flip.
  • Would you reach for it on night 14? Novelty is easy. Habit is the test.

We'll publish what we learn. If the rotation test fails, we redesign or kill the idea. If it passes, the next gate is 30 strangers, not friends.

Honest answers

How is this different from a $35 knee pillow sleeve?

Padded sleeves on the market put the cushion inside the sleeve, against the inner thigh of the worn leg. Plumb holds the cushion in an integrated pouch on the outside of the sleeve, so the cushion sits in the inter-thigh gap rather than being squeezed against your own skin. That's the bet. Whether it actually works better is what the wear test will tell us.

Will it cut off circulation?

The sleeve is soft knit and stretches significantly — closer to the feel of a wide compression sock than a strap. The grip comes from silicone dots, not from tightness. If a tester reports any numbness, tingling, or skin marks, we redesign. That's a hard kill criterion.

What happens when I roll over?

Honest answer: we don't fully know yet, and we're testing it. Here's the design response: the sleeve is wide (3+ inches) so a side-load force from the cushion is spread across more thigh contact area than a narrow strap would offer. The silicone dot grippers are placed top and bottom of the sleeve's interior so they engage skin (or sleepwear) in two zones, which we believe resists rotation as well as vertical slide. The cushion's concave base wraps the sleeve along its width, adding a third anchor point along the cushion-sleeve seam. None of this is proven yet — the wear test answers it with time-lapse video. If rotation does fail, the plan is to redesign the gripper layout and sleeve tension, not to ship something that doesn't work.

Which leg do I wear it on?

The dominant leg — the one that ends up on top more often when you're on your preferred side. Wear it on the leg that most often rests on top.

How tight is it?

Snug enough to stay in place; loose enough that you forget you're wearing it. The sleeve has at least three inches of width to distribute pressure across a wide area rather than concentrating it in a strap.

Is it washable?

Yes — the foam cushion slides out of the pouch on the sleeve exterior before washing. Machine-wash the sleeve cold and air-dry it flat, like any knit garment. Air the foam separately and replace it when it loses shape.

Does it sleep hot?

The sleeve and cushion exterior are a viscose-made-from-bamboo knit, targeted at the breathability profile typical of viscose-blend sleepwear. Per FTC textile labeling guidance, the fiber is described as "viscose made from bamboo," not "bamboo" alone. Third-party thermal testing is planned before launch, so we're not going to make a quantitative heat claim yet. What we can say: anything worn on the leg adds some thermal mass, and the wear test will tell us how much.

How do I pick a size?

Three sleeve sizes — Regular (17–22 in.), Large (22–27 in.), XL (27–32 in.) — based on upper thigh circumference. Measure around the fullest part of your upper thigh, about 3 inches below the crease. If you're between sizes, size down — the sleeve needs to be snug enough for the silicone dot grippers to engage. If you're below the bottom of the Regular range, email us before ordering and we'll talk through fit. Cushion thickness is a separate choice based on body size and mattress firmness.

Can I use it if I already have hip or back pain?

Plumb is a sleep-positioning product designed for comfort. It is not a medical device. It is not intended to diagnose, treat, mitigate, or cure any condition. If you have hip, back, or joint pain, talk to your doctor or physical therapist about whether a between-the-knees cushion is appropriate for you.

When can I buy one?

Not yet. Pre-prototype. If the wear test passes, we'll open a waitlist and ship the first production run later in 2026.

What if I don't love it?

Plumb is being designed around a generous home trial — the working target is 90 nights with free returns both ways. Final trial terms will be published before launch, after wear testing and production economics are complete. The intent is simple: if Plumb isn't the one for you, you should be able to send it back without friction.
